01. Order changes the learning problem

Timing: 00:00–02:00 (2 minutes).

Teaching intent: An observation becomes a sequence when its meaning depends on what came before it.

Open with a sensor trace, a sentence, and an ordered process log. Ask students why shuffling any of them may change the task. The trace is illustrative synthetic data, not a measured experiment. State today’s three-hour arc: stateful memory, selective retrieval, then a complete Transformer.

03. The same values can tell different stories

Timing: 04:00–06:00 (2 minutes).

Teaching intent: A sequence contains both values and an ordering rule.

Both series contain identical values and have the same mean, but the trend and one-step prediction problem differ. Ask students what a bag-of-values representation loses. Do not imply every prediction task requires order.

Ask: What remains identical after shuffling?

Expected answer: The multiset and mean; adjacency, trend, and time-dependent structure can change.

30. Exit: explain the compression problem

Timing: 58:00–60:00 (2 minutes).

Teaching intent: A final-state encoder compresses many inputs into one fixed-width vector.

Ask students to show the baseline table, gate parity assertion, and evidence JSON. Then ask what a decoder loses if it receives only the final state. This motivates querying all encoder states in H2; not every recurrent architecture uses only a final state.

Ask: What should H2 let each output retrieve?

Expected answer: The relevant earlier representations, with weights depending on the current output query.

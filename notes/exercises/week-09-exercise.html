Setup — predeclare a fair comparison

Independent synthetic inputs: $x_j\sim N(0,1)$; $y=2x_0+x_1^2+\epsilon$, noise SD 0.2. Three raw inputs are dimensionless; the output is in synthetic response units. These are neither stock data nor equipment measurements. Split exactly 350 train / 75 validation / 75 test. Fit every scaler on train only. Two candidates and validation MAE selection (ties favor linear) are declared before any test score. The two-model test comparison is a predeclared diagnosis, not permission to reselect.

Quick review (≤5 min): residual = prediction − observation; MAE averages absolute residuals, RMSE square-roots mean squared residuals. Both have response units. Neither a single average nor a single residual plot separates all error sources.

def truth(X): return 2*X[:,0]+X[:,1]**2
def sample(seed,n=500):
    rng=np.random.default_rng(seed); X=rng.normal(size=(n,3)); noise=rng.normal(0,.2,n)
    return X,truth(X)+noise,noise

train,val,test=map(np.sort,(train,val,test))

assert not(set(train)&set(val) or set(train)&set(test) or set(val)&set(test))

# The probe is fixed and noise-free: it isolates training-sample variability.
probe=np.random.default_rng(3001).normal(size=(240,3)); target=truth(probe)
sizes=[40,160,640]; repeats=20; decomposition=[]; predictions={}
for size in sizes:
    for kind in models:
        P=[]
        for r in range(repeats):
            # Prefixes make the two model families and sample sizes paired within a repetition.
            xr,yr,_=sample(4000+r,640)
            fit=model(kind).fit(xr[:size],yr[:size]);P.append(fit.predict(probe))
        P=np.array(P);mean=P.mean(axis=0)
        bias2=float(np.mean((mean-target)**2));variance=float(np.mean((P-mean)**2))
        signal_mse=float(np.mean((P-target)**2))
        assert np.isclose(signal_mse,bias2+variance,atol=1e-12)
        decomposition.append({'kind':kind,'n':size,'empirical_bias2':bias2,'refit_variance':variance,'signal_mse':signal_mse,'noise_averaged_mse':signal_mse+.04})
        predictions[kind,size]=P
print(json.dumps(decomposition,indent=2))

Interpret the mechanism after execution

For population unregularized least squares under the stated independent Gaussian inputs, the best affine projection is $1+2x_0$. Thus $E[e\mid x_1]=1-x_1^2$. This is a population statement, not the exact finite Ridge fit. Its population MSE is $Var(x_1^2)+Var(\epsilon)=2+0.04=2.04$; the correct quadratic oracle has MSE 0.04.

The repeated-fit array obeys an exact finite empirical identity: mean squared error to the known signal = squared error of the mean predictor + mean squared deviation around that mean (variance uses ddof=0). Adding 0.04 gives the noise-averaged expected component; it need not equal a single noisy test score. Twenty refits provide a teaching estimate, not a guarantee that all curves are monotone.

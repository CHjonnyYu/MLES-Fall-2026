HW9 · Diagnose and defend a change

Individual Homework · approximately two hours · 100 points

Due: Sunday, 8 November 2026, 23:59 Asia/Taipei (UTC+8).

Your task is to transfer the Week09 diagnostic method to a new engineering-data case. Identify one validation failure condition, propose and implement one controlled modeling change, then explain the actual result. A failed improvement is acceptable when the experiment and reasoning are sound.

What you must independently decide

Defend the target, split, baseline, evaluation and limitations. In particular, explain why the response is a temperature difference, which observations support your chosen failure condition, what your code change expresses, and which evidence would be needed for a broader claim.

Work through the starter in this order

  1. Read the contract. Save your own notebook copy. Keep the 600 rows and supplied split IDs fixed. Confirm units and the 360/120/120 counts.
  2. Diagnose on validation. Preserve StandardScaler → Ridge(alpha=1, SVD) as baseline. Inspect residuals against power and airflow. Choose one declared group: low airflow≤1 m/s, high power≥75 W, or hot ambient≥28°C. Report its count and metric; do not invent a failure from an average alone.
  3. Predict before changing code. Write your hypothesis, expected result, held-fixed conditions and failure group. Make one X-only feature-basis change or one active model-setting change. A feature function preserves original columns, uses no labels or IDs, and must be finite, deterministic and non-mutating. An unchanged identity, comment or random-seed edit does not complete the task.
  4. Compare fairly on validation. Use identical training rows and training-only preprocessing. Keep the baseline. The declared choice uses validation MAE; ties preserve baseline. The candidate may use a derived feature, a different Ridge alpha, or a small declared decision tree. Do not change both basis and model settings at once.
  5. Freeze, then inspect test. Save the code/configuration/plan identity before exposing the predeclared baseline/candidate test comparison. Explain overall MAE/RMSE and the selected group with its count. Do not reselect on test. Disclose any repeated test use as exploratory.
  6. Explain and export. Complete the five explanation fields and AI disclosure. Export to a fresh run directory, inspect the two charts and source in the ZIP, and save all three submission files. Retain this completed HW9 package for HW10.

The shared case: equipment temperature rise

The fixed CSV contains 600 independent synthetic equipment experiments, with a supplied 360/120/120 train/validation/test split. The rows are constructed for teaching; they are not measurements or validation of a real thermal system.

Column Meaning Unit and rule
run_id Stable experiment identity Unique string; not an ML feature
power_W Applied power W
airflow_m_s Airflow condition m/s
ambient_C Ambient condition °C
temp_rise_C Target: temperature rise above ambient °C difference; not absolute device temperature
split Frozen membership train, validation, or test; not an ML feature

The declared bounds check input encoding; they are not equipment safety limits. The contract records units, split counts and a digest of ID-keyed records. Random splitting is justified by this constructed independent-row assumption. It does not justify random splitting of a real time series. Scaling fits train only; validation selects; test provides a predeclared diagnosis after the proposal is frozen.

QUESTION 1 ·20 POINTS ·12 MINUTES

Frame a vibration prediction experiment

A pump dataset contains repeated runs from 18 pumps. At the start of a run, load R in percent, rotation speed U in thousands of revolutions per minute, and ambient temperature are known. Predict vibration RMS ten seconds later in mm/s. Deployment concerns previously unseen pumps. X contains only these available inputs; pump IDs are stored separately.

Z = StandardScaler().fit_transform(X)
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
    Z, y, test_size=0.25, random_state=12)

(a) ·4 points. State the observational unit, target, prediction time and type of supervised task.

(b) ·6 points. Give two problem→repair bullets: (i) pump membership across splits and (ii) preprocessing scope. Include the role of validation and when the final comparison is frozen; no library syntax is required.

(c) ·4 points. A separate RAW-input model predicts RMS = 0.5 + 0.05R + 0.2U. For R = 30, U = 2 and observed RMS = 3, calculate the prediction, signed residual (prediction minus observation), absolute error and squared error.

(d) ·6 points. Name a training-only constant baseline suited to RMSE; state one fair-comparison rule; then give one Ridge effect and one evaluation problem Ridge cannot repair.

Answer format. Use four labels in a; two problem→repair bullets in b; four values in c; three short bullets in d. About 70 prose words plus calculations are enough.

Show worked solution and scoring

Part a ·4 points

  • 1 points. One pump run is a row; runs within a pump can be dependent.
  • 1 points. Target is vibration RMS ten seconds later in mm/s.
  • 1 points. The prediction is issued at run start with then-available inputs.
  • 1 points. This is supervised regression.

Part b ·6 points

  • 1 points. Identify shared pump identities under a random row split.
  • 1 points. Keep whole pump IDs disjoint for unseen-pump deployment.
  • 1 points. Identify fitting the scaler before splitting as held-out information use.
  • 1 points. Fit the scaler on training rows only; apply that fitted transformation elsewhere.
  • 1 points. Use validation to choose settings.
  • 1 points. Freeze before testing on the untouched pump groups.

Part c ·4 points

  • 1 points. Prediction = 0.5 + 0.05(30) + 0.2(2) = 2.4 mm/s.
  • 1 points. Signed residual = 2.4 - 3 = -0.6 mm/s.
  • 1 points. Absolute error = 0.6 mm/s.
  • 1 points. Squared error = 0.36 (mm/s) squared.

Part d ·6 points

  • 2 points. Use the training-target mean as the squared-loss-optimal constant. Accept another explicitly training-only constant as a justified comparator; do not claim that a median must minimize RMSE.
  • 2 points. Compare on the same held-out pump groups, metric and permitted information.
  • 1 points. Ridge penalizes coefficient size and trades fit against regularization; a larger penalty does not guarantee a smaller absolute value for every individual coefficient.
  • 1 points. Regularization does not repair group overlap or globally fitted preprocessing.

Marking cautions

  • A different random seed does not separate pump identities.

QUESTION 3 ·10 POINTS ·7 MINUTES

Relate variance retention to prediction

Two standardized pump features have covariance [[1, 0.6], [0.6, 1]]. The supplied eigenvalues are 1.6 and 0.4. Unit eigenvectors are shown below. Consider z = (2, 0). Scores are dot products; PC1-only reconstruction is score 1 times v1.

v₁=(1, 1)/√2; v₂=(1,−1)/√2

(a) ·2 points. Calculate PC1’s retained-variance fraction.

(b) ·2 points. Calculate z’s PC1 score and reconstruction using PC1 alone.

(c) ·2 points. A fault is identified by the difference between the standardized features. Explain what PC1-only reconstruction can remove, even if it retains most variance.

(d) ·4 points. In a SEPARATE K-means fit on raw coordinates, what changes if one coordinate is converted from seconds to milliseconds without rescaling? Why do cluster IDs alone not identify physical fault modes?

Answer format. Use one fraction, one score and one reconstruction; then three short mechanism statements. About 35 prose words plus calculations are enough.

Show worked solution and scoring

Part a ·2 points

  • 1 points. Total input variance =1.6+0.4 =2.
  • 1 points. Retained fraction =1.6/2 =0.80 or 80%.

Part b ·2 points

  • 1 points. PC1 score = 2/sqrt(2) = sqrt(2).
  • 1 points. PC1-only reconstruction = (1, 1).

Part c ·2 points

  • 1 points. The discarded residual is z−reconstruction =(1,−1), in the feature-difference direction.
  • 1 points. That direction can carry label information;80% retained input variance does not guarantee retention of the fault signal.

Part d ·4 points

  • 1 points. The coordinate differences become 1000times larger.
  • 1 points. Their squared-distance contribution becomes 10^6 times larger, changing the clustering objective. Actual cluster assignments may or may not change.
  • 1 points. Cluster IDs are arbitrary labels for geometric groups.
  • 1 points. Physical fault interpretation requires independent evidence or validation.

Marking cautions

  • The reconstruction is not (2, 0); the PC2 component has been dropped.

QUESTION 4 ·25 POINTS ·19 MINUTES

Reason about a causal sequence model

Use the LSTM equations below. All gate outputs and the candidate are supplied. Attention logits are already scaled, immediately before masking and softmax.

ct=ftct−1+itgt
ht=ottanh(ct)

(a) ·4 points. For x0=1,x1=3,x2=2,x3=5,x4=4, use a four-value lookback at time 3 to predict x4. Give input and target, why x4 is unavailable, and one chronological split rule.

(b) ·6 points. Given c_previous = 1, f_t = 0.6, i_t = 0.5, g_t = 0.8 and o_t = 0.25, calculate c_t and h_t using tanh(1) = 0.7616. Then set ONLY o_t to zero and explain what happens to the hidden output and the stored cell state.

(c) ·8 points. Scaled logits are [ln(2),0,ln(3)] with exponentials [2, 1, 3]. Key 2 is padding; keys are numbered 1–3. V1=[5, 0],V2=[100, 100],V3=[0, 5]. Give masked normalized weights and context. Explain why only zeroing the padded weight AFTER unmasked softmax is insufficient.

(d) ·7 points. A fixed deterministic causal decoder receives two length-10 inputs with identical positions 0–3 and changed positions 4–9; position indices and all other inputs are unchanged. Answer three rows: (i) which logits must agree, and why; (ii) why teacher-forced training can be parallel but ordinary generation is sequential; (iii) what explicit positional information adds beyond the causal mask.

Answer format. Use the requested values and short labeled statements. In d answer three rows: prefix check, training/generation, positions. About 70 prose words plus calculations are enough.

Show worked solution and scoring

Part a ·4 points

  • 1 points. Input [1, 3, 2, 5].
  • 1 points. Target x4 = 4.
  • 1 points. x4 is future and unavailable at time 3.
  • 1 points. Assign targets chronologically and construct all features from past/available information only; historical context can be used when legitimately known.

Part b ·6 points

  • 2 points. Cell = 0.6(1)+0.5(0.8) = 1.0.
  • 2 points. Hidden = 0.25(0.7616) = 0.1904.
  • 2 points. Closing only the output gate gives hidden = 0, cell = 1 at this step. It does not erase memory, and later dynamics need not be unchanged.

Part c ·8 points

  • 1 points. Exclude key 2: valid exponentials [2, 0, 3]; the masked logit is−infinity.
  • 2 points. Valid denominator 5 gives weights [2/5, 0, 3/5].
  • 1 points. Use the weighted sum of valid values.
  • 2 points. Context =(2/5)[5, 0]+(3/5)[0, 5] =[2, 3].
  • 1 points. Zeroing after ordinary softmax gives [2/6, 0, 3/6], sum 5/6.
  • 1 points. Renormalize over valid keys. Zeroing AND renormalizing is also correct; zeroing alone is not.

Part d ·7 points

  • 3 points. Positions 0–3 agree (1). They cannot access the changed suffix (1), and their available prefix/other inputs are identical (1). Later logits need not differ.
  • 2 points. Teacher forcing supplies all shifted input tokens, permitting masked parallel training (1). Ordinary generation needs previous generated tokens for the next step (1).
  • 2 points. Explicit positions give location/order/distance cues (1); causal masking restricts future access (1). Some causal models infer implicit positions; do not require an order-blind-without-explicit-positions claim.

Marking cautions

  • The middle key, not the last key, is padding here.
  • Unchanged prefixes must agree; changed suffix logits need not differ. Masking and explicit positions have distinct roles.

At one fixed input, the known noise-free target is 2.0. The controlled synthetic study has independent zero-mean observation-noise variance 0.25. The table summarizes 20 independent training resamples. Treat its reported moments as the supplied estimates. Use expected MSE = squared bias + refit variance + noise variance; do not take a square root.

ModelTraining rowsMean predictionRefit variance
Affine801.60.25
Affine8001.60.01
Nonlinear8002.00.08

(a) ·6 points. Calculate squared bias and expected MSE for the 800-row affine model, and expected MSE for the 800-row nonlinear model.

(b) ·4 points. Use the two affine rows to state what more data changed and what it did not. Explain one possible cause of a persistent curved mean residual and what happens to the stated observation-noise floor.

(c) ·4 points. Distinguish A, deployment humidity values become more frequent in a different range with the same conditional response, from B, pump ageing changes the conditional response at the same inputs.

(d) ·6 points. A frozen model has MAE 3 on 20 high-load cases and MAE 0.5 on 80 other test cases. Compute total test MAE and its reweighted value when high-load cases become 40 percent of deployment, assuming within-group mean absolute errors stay fixed. Explain the assumption and one limitation.

Answer format. Show the short calculations; answer b–d in compact bullets. About 65 prose words plus calculations are enough.

Show worked solution and scoring

Part a ·6 points

  • 2 points. Bias = 1.6 - 2.0 = -0.4; squared bias = 0.16.
  • 2 points. Affine MSE = 0.16 + 0.01 + 0.25 = 0.42.
  • 2 points. Nonlinear MSE = 0 + 0.08 + 0.25 = 0.33.

Part b ·4 points

  • 1 points. Affine refit variance falls from 0.25 to 0.01.
  • 1 points. Mean prediction remains 1.6, so the estimated bias at this input remains−0.4.
  • 1 points. A persistent curved mean residual can suggest model-form misspecification; more rows do not enlarge the affine hypothesis class. The curve alone is not a unique proof of the mechanism.
  • 1 points. The stated independent noise floor remains 0.25 under the same available information. More training rows do not remove it.

Part c ·4 points

  • 2 points. A is input/covariate shift with fixed P(Y given X).
  • 2 points. B changes P(Y given X); unchanged marginal inputs would not prevent this relationship shift.

Part d ·6 points

  • 2 points. Test MAE = 0.2(3)+0.8(0.5) = 1.0.
  • 2 points. Reweighted MAE = 0.4(3)+0.6(0.5) = 1.5.
  • 1 points. The group-conditional mean absolute errors must remain stable.
  • 1 points. Changes within a group, target-response/noise changes or subgroup sampling uncertainty can invalidate the estimate. It is not direct deployment validation.

Marking cautions

  • MSE and RMSE are not interchangeable in the decomposition.

Examiner follow-up — show after the main answer

Show reasoning, model response and scoring
CriterionPointsCase-specific anchors

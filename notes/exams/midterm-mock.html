QUESTION 1 · 20 POINTS · 14 MINUTES

Frame a vibration prediction experiment

A pump dataset contains repeated runs from 18 pumps. At the start of a run, load R in percent, rotation speed U in thousands of revolutions per minute, and ambient temperature are known. Predict vibration RMS ten seconds later in mm/s. Deployment concerns previously unseen pumps. X contains only these available inputs; pump IDs are stored separately.

Z = StandardScaler().fit_transform(X)
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
    Z, y, test_size=0.25, random_state=12)

(a) · 4 points. State the observational unit, target, prediction time and type of supervised task.

(b) · 6 points. Identify two evaluation problems for unseen-pump deployment. Describe a corrected train, validation and test procedure; exact library syntax is unnecessary.

(c) · 4 points. A separate RAW-input model predicts RMS = 0.5 + 0.05R + 0.2U. For R = 30, U = 2 and observed RMS = 3, calculate the prediction, signed residual (prediction minus observation), absolute error and squared error.

(d) · 6 points. Propose a leakage-free constant baseline for RMSE, describe a fair comparison, and explain what increasing a Ridge penalty can control and what it cannot fix in part b.

Show worked solution and scoring

Part a · 4 points

  • 1 point. One pump run is a row; runs within a pump can be dependent.
  • 1 point. Target is vibration RMS ten seconds later in mm/s.
  • 1 point. The prediction is issued at run start with then-available inputs.
  • 1 point. This is supervised regression.

Part b · 6 points

  • 2 points. Random row splits can share pump identities across partitions and fail to represent new-pump deployment.
  • 2 points. The global scaler uses held-out distribution information.
  • 2 points. Partition whole pump IDs into disjoint train, validation and test groups; fit preprocessing on train, select on validation, freeze and test. Accept a justified combined time/group protocol that preserves unseen-pump evaluation.

Part c · 4 points

  • 1 point. Prediction = 0.5 + 0.05(30) + 0.2(2) = 2.4 mm/s.
  • 1 point. Signed residual = 2.4 - 3 = -0.6 mm/s.
  • 1 point. Absolute error = 0.6 mm/s.
  • 1 point. Squared error = 0.36 (mm/s) squared.

Part d · 6 points

  • 2 points. The training target mean is the standard squared-loss constant baseline. Accept another explicitly training-only constant, including a median, with a defensible comparator claim; it need not be RMSE-optimal. Held-out targets must not determine it.
  • 2 points. Compare on identical held-out pump IDs using the same RMSE definition and permitted information.
  • 1 point. A stronger Ridge penalty shrinks coefficients and can trade variance for bias.
  • 1 point. It does not repair pump overlap or globally fitted preprocessing.

Common mistakes

  • A different random seed does not separate pump identities.

QUESTION 3 · 10 POINTS · 7 MINUTES

Relate variance retention to prediction

Two standardized pump features have covariance [[1, 0.6], [0.6, 1]]. The supplied eigenvalues are 1.6 and 0.4. Unit eigenvectors are shown below. Consider z = (2, 0). Scores are dot products; PC1-only reconstruction is score1 times v1.

v1 = (1, 1)/√2   v2 = (1, −1)/√2

(a) · 2 points. Calculate PC1’s retained-variance fraction.

(b) · 2 points. Calculate z’s PC1 score and reconstruction using PC1 alone.

(c) · 2 points. A fault is identified by the difference between the standardized features. Explain what PC1-only reconstruction can remove, even if it retains most variance.

(d) · 4 points. Explain how changing an unscaled coordinate from seconds to milliseconds can affect K-means, and why cluster numbers do not automatically name real fault modes.

Show worked solution and scoring

Part a · 2 points

  • 2 points. 1.6/(1.6+0.4) = 0.80 or 80 percent.

Part b · 2 points

  • 1 point. PC1 score = 2/sqrt(2) = sqrt(2).
  • 1 point. PC1-only reconstruction = (1, 1).

Part c · 2 points

  • 2 points. The discarded residual z - reconstruction = (1, -1) lies in PC2, the feature-difference direction. High retained input variance does not guarantee retention of the label signal.

Part d · 4 points

  • 2 points. A factor-1000 change changes squared Euclidean distances and the raw-space clustering objective; use justified units/scaling.
  • 2 points. Cluster IDs are arbitrary names for geometric groups. Physical fault labels require independent validation.

Common mistakes

  • The reconstruction is not (2,0); the PC2 component has been dropped.

QUESTION 4 · 25 POINTS · 19 MINUTES

Reason about a causal sequence model

Use the LSTM equations below. All gate outputs and the candidate are supplied. Attention logits are already scaled, immediately before masking and softmax.

ct = ftct−1 + itgt
ht = ot tanh(ct)

(a) · 4 points. A series has x0 = 1, x1 = 3, x2 = 2, x3 = 5, x4 = 4. At time 3 use a four-value lookback to predict x4. Give the input and target, identify why x4 cannot enter the input, and state the split discipline for predicting a future time period.

(b) · 6 points. Given ct−1 = 1, ft = 0.6, it = 0.5, gt = 0.8 and ot = 0.25, calculate ct and ht using tanh(1) = 0.7616. Then set ONLY ot to zero and explain what happens to the hidden output and the stored cell state.

(c) · 8 points. Logits are [ln(2), 0, ln(3)], with exponentials [2, 1, 3]. Key 2 is padding. V1 = [5,0], V2 = [100,100], V3 = [0,5]. Compute masked normalized weights and context. Explain why zeroing the padding weight after an ordinary unmasked softmax is insufficient.

(d) · 7 points. Two length-10 inputs to a fixed deterministic causal decoder share positions 0 through 3 and differ at positions 4 through 9. Which output logits must agree and why? Explain parallel teacher-forced training versus ordinary sequential autoregressive generation, and the purpose of position information.

Show worked solution and scoring

Part a · 4 points

  • 1 point. Input [1,3,2,5].
  • 1 point. Target x4 = 4.
  • 1 point. x4 is future and unavailable at time 3.
  • 1 point. Assign targets chronologically and construct all features from past/available information only; historical context can be used when legitimately known.

Part b · 6 points

  • 2 points. Cell = 0.6(1)+0.5(0.8) = 1.0.
  • 2 points. Hidden = 0.25(0.7616) = 0.1904.
  • 2 points. Closing only the output gate gives hidden = 0, cell = 1 at this step. It does not erase memory, and later dynamics need not be unchanged.

Part c · 8 points

  • 3 points. Masked exponentials [2,0,3], normalized weights [2/5,0,3/5].
  • 3 points. Context = (2/5)[5,0]+(3/5)[0,5] = [2,3].
  • 2 points. Unmasked softmax gives [2/6,1/6,3/6]; zeroing the middle entry leaves a sum of 5/6. Normalize over valid keys. Padding and causal masks address different exclusions.

Part d · 7 points

  • 2 points. Output logits at positions 0 through 3 must agree within the agreed numerical tolerance.
  • 1 point. Each causal position can access only its own prefix; padding exclusion alone does not enforce that.
  • 2 points. Teacher forcing supplies known shifted inputs at all positions, permitting masked parallel operations; generation requires previously generated inputs.
  • 2 points. Position information gives ordering information absent from content-only attention; absolute or relative mechanisms can provide it.

Common mistakes

  • The middle key, not the last key, is padding here.

(b) · 4 points. Explain what the larger sample changes and what it does not fix. Relate this to a persistent curved mean residual and to observation noise.

(c) · 4 points. Distinguish A, deployment humidity values become more frequent in a different range with the same conditional response, from B, pump ageing changes the conditional response at the same inputs.

Part a · 6 points

  • 2 points. Bias = 1.6 - 2.0 = -0.4; squared bias = 0.16.
  • 2 points. Affine MSE = 0.16 + 0.01 + 0.25 = 0.42.
  • 2 points. Nonlinear MSE = 0 + 0.08 + 0.25 = 0.33.

Part b · 4 points

  • 2 points. Refit variance decreases from 0.25 to 0.01 while mean prediction/bias stay fixed.
  • 1 point. A curved mean residual can reflect a missing nonlinear relationship; more samples do not change the affine hypothesis class.
  • 1 point. Independent observation noise persists under the stated information assumptions.

Part c · 4 points

  • 2 points. A is input/covariate shift with fixed P(Y given X).
  • 2 points. B changes P(Y given X); unchanged marginal inputs would not prevent this relationship shift.

Part d · 6 points

  • 2 points. Test MAE = 0.2(3)+0.8(0.5) = 1.0.
  • 2 points. Reweighted MAE = 0.4(3)+0.6(0.5) = 1.5.
  • 1 point. The group-conditional mean absolute errors must remain stable.
  • 1 point. Changes within a group, target-response/noise changes or subgroup sampling uncertainty can invalidate the estimate. It is not direct deployment validation.

Sequence model clarifications

For Mock Question 4, mask the padded key before softmax and normalize over the remaining keys. Exponentials [2, 0, 3] therefore produce weights [0.4, 0, 0.6]. A padding mask removes nonexistent positions; a causal mask restricts access to future positions.

In the stated deterministic causal check, preserve positions 0–3 and change only 4–9. Logits at positions 0–3 must remain unchanged. Closing only the current LSTM output gate makes the hidden output zero while leaving the current stored cell state unchanged; later dynamics may still change.

ORAL PRACTICE M1 · TWO MINUTES

Selection after test

A student inspects the test RMSE, changes Ridge alpha five times, then reports the smallest test RMSE as the final unbiased result.

Main question. Identify the main evaluation problem and propose a corrected next step.

Follow-up. Would fixing the random seed make the final test claim valid?

Show reasoning and claim limits

Decision. The test was used for model selection, so the reported minimum is not an untouched final estimate. Use training/validation for development, freeze the comparison and obtain new untouched final test evidence when justified.

Evidence. Five alpha choices were selected by the same test scores. The controlled procedure must preserve a baseline, declared metric and comparison population.

Counterfactual. A fixed seed improves repeatability of a computation, but does not undo information use or selection bias.

Limitation. A reproducible optimistic result can still be an invalid generalization estimate.

ORAL PRACTICE M2 · TWO MINUTES

A unit label in a replay

A replay package expects airflow in m/s. A copied contract says km/h while retaining the same numerical inputs. The loader rejects the contract before fitting.

Main question. Is this rejection useful or a model failure? Explain the next appropriate action.

Follow-up. If you simply rename km/h back to m/s and obtain matching predictions, have you established that the original measurements were correctly encoded?

Show reasoning and claim limits

Decision. This is useful contract enforcement before model fitting, not evidence that the fitted model failed. Diagnose the actual original units and make any justified conversion explicit and reviewed.

Evidence. Relabelled unchanged numbers change the interpretation. Keep the original artifact and record the failed attempt, correction and actual retest.

Counterfactual. No. Matching numbers after relabelling can reproduce an encoding error. Verify measurement provenance and units; do not silently alter metadata to force agreement.

Limitation. Computational agreement does not establish the physical meaning of the original inputs.

A strong response identifies the decision, cites the supplied evidence and explains a bounded next step. A fixed seed can repeat a computation but does not repair test-driven selection. Use the worked-solutions download for full scoring guidance and sample responses.

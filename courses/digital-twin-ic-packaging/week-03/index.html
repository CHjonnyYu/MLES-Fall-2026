Course introduction and package-to-evidence thinking

Explain what an advanced package is, why processes and design interact, and how this course evaluates engineering claims.

How this week works: Three approximately 56-minute modules plus two short transitions. Each hour has a distinct teaching target and a reviewable evidence checkpoint. InPack.AI is the course analysis platform; the handout records assumptions and outputs without inventing a software feature.

Public case: Public architecture and roadmap examples

TSMC's public packaging page gives architecture context; the IEEE heterogeneous-integration roadmap frames co-design. Classroom package dimensions and results are explicitly synthetic.

Evidence boundary: A public service description is not a disclosed process recipe or a measurement of our class case.

Teaching modules

H1 · What is inside an advanced package?

Outcome: Identify die, interconnect, substrate, and thermal path on a package cross-section.

Evidence to keep: An annotated package cross-section and a list of unknowns.

Open H1 slidesSource notes

Die and package have different jobs

The die computes; the package connects, supports, protects, and removes heat.

Worked reasoning: Trace a signal and a heat path from a compute die to the board and ambient.

Check your understanding: Can transistor design alone specify a package?

No. Interfaces, materials, assembly, and thermal boundaries remain.

Sources: R1 R2

Heterogeneous integration changes the map

Multiple dies can divide functions but add die-to-die links and assembly interfaces.

Worked reasoning: Use a conceptual compute-plus-memory layout; name one bandwidth benefit and one routing cost without inventing performance numbers.

Check your understanding: Does adding a chiplet automatically improve the system?

No. Link, power, thermal, yield, and cost constraints must be assessed.

Sources: R1 R2

Follow one complete connection

A logical connection becomes pads, bumps, traces, vias, and a return path.

Worked reasoning: Draw one memory signal from the compute die through an interposer or RDL to the memory die.

Check your understanding: What is missing from a simple arrow between dies?

Physical pitch, routing layers, losses, and return path.

Sources: R2 R3

Name the architecture before judging it

2D, 2.5D, and 3D arrangements offer different interconnect and thermal constraints.

Worked reasoning: Classify three schematic arrangements by geometry, not by marketing name.

Check your understanding: Is every multi-die package 3D?

No. Vertical stacking is a distinct geometry.

Sources: R1 R3

H2 · From fabrication to assembly

Outcome: Order major package operations and name the state each operation changes.

Evidence to keep: A process map with inspection points and stage-dependent outputs.

Open H2 slidesSource notes

Start with prepared die and surfaces

Dicing, surface preparation, and placement define interfaces that later process steps cannot ignore.

Worked reasoning: Mark where contamination or misalignment could enter before bonding.

Check your understanding: Can a later simulation erase an unknown surface state?

No. It needs an explicit assumption or measurement.

Sources: R1 R3

Bonding creates a mechanical interface

Bumps, solder, or direct bonding connect dies while introducing alignment and material constraints.

Worked reasoning: Compare a bumped interface with a conceptual direct-bond interface; list distinct inspection needs.

Check your understanding: Does electrical continuity prove bond integrity?

No. It is one check under one condition.

Sources: R3 R12

Encapsulation changes stress history

Molding and cure can create thermal mismatch and residual deformation.

Worked reasoning: Sketch the shape before and after cooling; state the reference temperature as unknown if absent.

Check your understanding: Can room-temperature shape reveal the entire cure history?

No. Process path and material evolution matter.

Sources: R7 R8

Inspect at the relevant gate

Metrology should occur when a failure can still be localized or a handling limit matters.

Worked reasoning: Choose a warpage check after debonding and explain why a final-only check can miss the earlier risk.

Check your understanding: Is the final inspection enough for every process decision?

No. Intermediate stages may have different limits.

Sources: R8 R12

H3 · Course evidence and assessment

Outcome: Separate published facts, model predictions, and physical validation in each course exercise.

Evidence to keep: A one-page evidence card for one package claim.

Open H3 slidesSource notes

A source supports a bounded claim

A source must match the claimed package, quantity, and conditions.

Worked reasoning: Use the public TSMC overview only for architecture context; do not extract an undisclosed stack thickness.

Check your understanding: Can a general web page supply a missing material curve?

No. The value and range need a specific traceable source.

Sources: R1 R6

Model output is a prediction

A simulation result depends on geometry, materials, loads, boundary conditions, and implementation.

Worked reasoning: A synthetic 80 µm warpage label is unusable until metric, stage, and temperature are defined.

Check your understanding: Is one attractive contour a validation result?

No. It lacks a matched physical reference and checks.

Sources: R6 R8

Homework practices the evidence chain

Course work asks students to state assumptions, perform a calculation, and explain what would test it.

Worked reasoning: For a two-layout comparison, write the held-fixed conditions and one measurement that could reverse the ranking.

Check your understanding: What makes an answer reviewable?

Traceable inputs, units, method, decision rule, and limits.

Sources: R2 R6

Exams combine written and oral reasoning

The midterm and final test definitions, calculations, and defensible engineering explanations.

Worked reasoning: Practice explaining why a lower predicted temperature may not win if a routing rule fails.

Check your understanding: Does a polished recommendation replace its evidence?

No. The reasoning and stated uncertainty must be examinable.

Sources: R2 R6

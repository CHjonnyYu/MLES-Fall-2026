Integrated chip-package co-design case

Compare two chiplet-package candidates across routing, thermal behavior, warpage, process constraints, and measurement needs.

How this week works: Three approximately 56-minute modules plus two short transitions. Each hour has a distinct teaching target and a reviewable evidence checkpoint. InPack.AI is the course analysis platform; the handout records assumptions and outputs without inventing a software feature.

Public case: IEEE co-design roadmap and public 2.5D thermal case

The IEEE roadmap provides the multi-domain design frame; a published two-chiplet thermal study provides a separate physical example. Course candidate A/B numbers are synthetic and are never presented as measured results.

Evidence boundary: Combining sources into a teaching scenario does not create a validated package or cross-paper numerical benchmark.

Teaching modules

H1 · Write the system brief

Outcome: Turn a system request into package variables and hard constraints before optimizing.

Evidence to keep: A requirement sheet with units, conditions, and unknowns.

Open H1 slidesSource notes

Define an intended use

A package design question needs a workload, interface, operating range, and decision criterion.

Worked reasoning: Synthetic two-chiplet brief: state power per die, link count, ambient, and maximum allowed temperature as classroom assumptions.

Check your understanding: Is 'better performance' a usable objective?

No. Specify metric, condition, and acceptance rule.

Sources: R2 R6

Separate hard limits from preferences

Manufacturing rule failures block a candidate even when another metric improves.

Worked reasoning: A layout with shorter links but illegal bump escape remains a redesign candidate.

Check your understanding: May a cooler layout pass an illegal routing rule?

No. Resolve the rule violation.

Sources: R2 R3

Choose decision variables

Placement, stackup, material, and cooling changes affect different physics and costs.

Worked reasoning: Hold the die and load fixed while moving one chiplet; then record the additional checks triggered.

Check your understanding: Can several changes be attributed to one cause?

No. Use controlled comparisons or a declared experiment design.

Sources: R2 R4

Record the baseline

A candidate comparison is reproducible only when the baseline model and assumptions have versioned identifiers.

Worked reasoning: Create a case record with geometry, materials, loads, boundary conditions, and output definitions.

Check your understanding: Does a screenshot preserve enough for replay?

Usually no. Preserve the actual input record and version.

Sources: R5 R6

H2 · Build comparable candidates

Outcome: Trace one change through electrical, thermal, and mechanical consequences.

Evidence to keep: A two-candidate matrix with matched inputs and explicit constraint checks.

Open H2 slidesSource notes

Placement changes more than distance

Moving a chiplet can shorten routes while changing heat coupling and substrate bending.

Worked reasoning: Contrast adjacent and separated chiplets at equal power and package outline; mark predicted effects as hypotheses.

Check your understanding: Does adjacency guarantee a cooler or faster system?

No. Analyze routing, power, cooling, and constraints.

Sources: R2 R4 R10

Cooling changes boundary conditions

TIM and flow rate are separate experimental variables in a two-chiplet thermal comparison.

Worked reasoning: Feng et al. report 56% and 76% reductions in thermal interaction with liquid-metal TIM versus DOW5888 at 0.2 and 0.8 L/min in their test vehicle. Explain why each percentage needs its flow condition.

Check your understanding: Can different coolant flows be treated as a placement effect?

No. Flow is a changed boundary condition; the reported reduction is vehicle-specific thermal interaction, not universal die-temperature reduction.

Sources: R4 R15

Warpage follows the process state

An architecture can look acceptable in service but exceed a handling limit during assembly.

Worked reasoning: For each candidate, specify whether warpage is after mold, debond, or final assembly and match the metric.

Check your understanding: Can final flatness clear an earlier process limit?

No. Check the relevant stage.

Sources: R8 R13

Do not conflate simulation and test

A predicted difference remains conditional until checked with matched physical evidence.

Worked reasoning: List the specimen and sensor needed to verify the candidate's claimed thermal advantage.

Check your understanding: Does agreement between two solvers prove physical accuracy?

No. It is an independent numerical comparison, not a physical measurement.

Sources: R6 R15

H3 · Choose and defend a next action

Outcome: Rank feasible candidates with uncertainty and choose a test that could reverse the decision.

Evidence to keep: A reviewable decision memo with an explicit next measurement.

Open H3 slidesSource notes

Screen feasibility before ranking

A constraint table should identify pass, fail, and unknown rather than hide missing evidence in a score.

Worked reasoning: Synthetic A passes thermal but has unknown routing; B passes routing but is warmer. Mark A unresolved, not a winner.

Check your understanding: Can unknown be treated as pass?

No. State the evidence needed.

Sources: R2 R6

Use matched output definitions

Compare the same temperature location, warpage sign and stage, and electrical frequency.

Worked reasoning: Reject two 'peak temperature' values if one is die junction and the other is interposer surface.

Check your understanding: Why can equal units still mislead?

They may refer to different locations or conditions.

Sources: R2 R6

Quantify decision sensitivity

A small ranking margin can be reversed by uncertain material or boundary values.

Worked reasoning: Synthetic A is 2 K cooler; test whether a plausible interface-resistance range reverses that margin.

Check your understanding: Does one nominal run settle a close choice?

No. Test the uncertainty that matters to the decision.

Sources: R4 R6

Write a bounded recommendation

State the use, evidence, constraints, uncertainty, and one discriminating next test.

Worked reasoning: Recommend an option only within the tested condition, with unresolved routing or measurement limits visible.

Check your understanding: What would invalidate the recommendation?

A failed hard rule or matched measurement that reverses the ranking.

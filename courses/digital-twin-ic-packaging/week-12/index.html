InPack.AI engineering workflow

Build an auditable model record: geometry, materials, process state, boundary conditions, outputs, versions, and missing evidence.

How this week works: Three approximately 56-minute modules plus two short transitions. Each hour has a distinct teaching target and a reviewable evidence checkpoint. InPack.AI is the course analysis platform; the handout records assumptions and outputs without inventing a software feature.

Public case: NIST credibility guidance applied to packaging

The NIST publication frames model credibility around intended use and supporting evidence. Students use that public framework to audit the instructor's InPack.AI case or export; no platform result is invented in these materials.

Evidence boundary: The NIST framework does not demonstrate that any specific InPack.AI prediction is accurate.

Teaching modules

H1 · Define the model before running it

Outcome: Specify a package, stage, output, and intended use before entering any geometry or material value.

Evidence to keep: A versioned input record with every quantity's unit and source.

Open H1 slidesSource notes

Name the physical package

A model needs an explicit architecture, dimensions, interconnect scheme, and process stage; a generic label is insufficient.

Worked reasoning: For a public fan-out warpage paper, record its specimen and stage separately from the course case. Mark dimensions absent from the class export as not supplied.

Check your understanding: Can a paper's geometry silently fill a course-case blank?

No. It is a different specimen unless the equivalence is documented.

Sources: R6 R8

Every material value needs context

CTE, modulus, conductivity, and cure state may change with temperature and processing; units and source are part of the input.

Worked reasoning: Audit one EMC modulus entry. Record whether it is a constant or a curve, the temperature range, and the measurement or supplier source.

Check your understanding: Is one room-temperature modulus enough for a reflow calculation?

Not without evidence that it represents the relevant range and state.

Sources: R4 R7

Boundary conditions define the problem

Constraints, thermal loads, symmetry, and reference state can dominate a result even with identical geometry.

Worked reasoning: Two synthetic warpage models use the same stack but different support conditions. Their outputs cannot be compared as a material effect.

Boundary: The two-model contrast is synthetic teaching data.

Check your understanding: What must be held fixed for a sensitivity test?

All other relevant geometry, loads, boundaries, output definitions, and solver settings.

Sources: R6 R8

Version inputs and decisions

A run record should preserve case ID, input revision, solver or method identifier, timestamp, and a reason for each changed value.

Worked reasoning: Before changing underfill conductivity, save the original value and output. The new run differs by one declared parameter.

Check your understanding: Can a screenshot alone reproduce the run?

Usually not. It omits some inputs, settings, units, and version information.

Sources: R5 R6

H2 · Interpret outputs without overclaiming

Outcome: Read output definitions and compare predictions with compatible observations.

Evidence to keep: An output table with quantity, location, stage, units, and a validation status.

Open H2 slidesSource notes

Define the reported quantity

Peak junction temperature, average die temperature, edge warpage, and center displacement are distinct outputs.

Worked reasoning: A course export says 'warpage 80 µm'. Ask whether this is peak-to-valley, center-to-edge, and at which temperature before ranking candidates.

Boundary: The 80 µm phrase is a synthetic ambiguity exercise.

Check your understanding: Can two files both saying 'warpage' be compared directly?

Only after definition, geometry, stage, and conditions match.

Sources: R6 R8

Compare like with like

A predicted value and measured value form a pair only when specimen, stage, load, location, and unit agree.

Worked reasoning: A measurement after debond cannot validate a prediction after grinding; construct matched pairs before computing residuals.

Check your understanding: What should an unmatched row say?

Not comparable; identify the missing or different condition.

Sources: R6 R8

Inspect local and global behavior

One maximum can conceal a shifted hot spot or a changed deformation mode; inspect the field and the quantity used for the decision.

Worked reasoning: Two synthetic layouts share the same temperature maximum but differ in where the hot spot touches the memory interface.

Boundary: The two-layout comparison is synthetic teaching data.

Check your understanding: Does equal peak temperature imply equal thermal risk?

No. Location, area, time, and neighboring components may differ.

Sources: R2 R4

Declare missing validation

A model output is a prediction until a suitable physical comparison or justified benchmark supports the intended use.

Worked reasoning: If no physical measurement is available, label the class result 'unvalidated for this use' and propose a coupon or package test.

Check your understanding: May a published validation from another package be reused?

Only with a justified transfer argument and stated limits; do not assume it automatically applies.

H3 · Complete the assumptions sheet

Outcome: Produce a reviewable case record that another student can audit from the public source and available export.

Evidence to keep: A model-input and assumptions sheet with one uncertainty ranking and next measurement.

Open H3 slidesSource notes

Record what is known and unknown

Separate observed input, inferred input, default, and missing field; their evidence strengths differ.

Worked reasoning: Mark an unspecified CTE curve as missing rather than copying a plausible value from another material system.

Check your understanding: Does a reasonable-looking number become observed evidence?

No. Provenance, material identity, and measurement conditions are required.

Sources: R6

Change one factor deliberately

A sensitivity comparison changes one declared input while preserving all other comparability conditions.

Worked reasoning: Change a synthetic underfill conductivity from 0.5 to 0.7 W/(m·K) only as an exercise; keep geometry and load fixed.

Boundary: Conductivities are synthetic teaching values.

Check your understanding: Can the output difference prove the true material value?

No. It measures model sensitivity under an assumed change, not the actual property.

Sources: R4 R6

Rank uncertainty by decision impact

A missing input matters most when plausible variation could reverse the candidate ordering or cross a hard limit.

Worked reasoning: Compare whether interface resistance or minor trace width uncertainty more strongly affects the thermal choice, then prioritize the first measurement.

Check your understanding: Must every uncertain input be measured first?

No. Prioritize by sensitivity, plausible range, and decision consequence.

Sources: R6

Export a narrow evidence card

The final record states a claim, case revision, conditions, output, uncertainty, and next physical check.

Worked reasoning: A peer should retrace the comparison from the saved case or export; if a key field is missing, the card says so explicitly.

Check your understanding: What would make the card fail peer review?

An untraceable number, mismatched conditions, omitted uncertainty, or a claim broader than the evidence.

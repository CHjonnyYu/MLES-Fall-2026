Public IC packaging cases

Seven research-grounded problems connect package processes, materials, design choices, and digital-twin evidence. Each case names its source and the limit of the available evidence.

Use in class: Read the linked paper, record the exact specimen and process stage, then answer the problem. Any extra classroom candidate values are labeled synthetic. A publication is not an InPack.AI output or NeuroFEM validation. The NIST credibility framework guides the evidence check.

Multi-die FOWLP: process-stage warpage

Simulation + measurement
Published source
Chen et al., Materials (2022), Tables 1–2, 7–9; Figures 8 and 11
Problem
Calculate the model–measurement error after PI2 cure, molding, and grinding. Explain why the errors change by stage, then propose one missing process input to test.
Numbers or observations to start from
Published pairs (simulation / experiment): 0.172 / 0.160 mm, 1.473 / 0.887 mm, 1.022 / 0.620 mm.
Evidence boundary
The later-stage discrepancy is substantial. A good early-stage fit does not validate the whole process.

NCKU EMC selection: one good fit, many sensitivities

Simulation + measurement
Published source
Chuang, Huang & Chen, Materials (2023), Tables 2–5, 7, 10
Problem
Compare the nominal measured and simulated decarrier warpage. Then change lower-temperature EMC CTE in the published parameter sweep and explain what the change does, and does not, establish.
Numbers or observations to start from
Published: 7.329 ± 0.3 mm measured; 7.34 mm simulated in X. The model sweep gives 7.340 mm at α₁ = 8 ppm/K and 6.431 mm at α₁ = 7 ppm/K.
Evidence boundary
The 0.15% nominal comparison is one state of one specimen. The parameter sweep is a model result, not a measured production improvement.

Two chiplets: self-heating and thermal interaction

Model + thermal test vehicle
Published source
Feng et al., Applied Sciences (2024), Figure 1 and cooling experiments
Problem
Draw heat paths from two dies through TIM, interposer, and sink. Design two power-loading experiments that would separate self-heating from cross-heating. Compare the two TIMs at matched flow.
Numbers or observations to start from
The authors report liquid-metal TIM reduced thermal interaction relative to DOW5888 by 56% at 0.2 L/min and 76% at 0.8 L/min for their test vehicle.
Evidence boundary
These are the authors’ vehicle-specific thermal-interaction comparisons, not universal die-temperature reductions.

TSV/RDL corner: thermal cycling and submodeling

Simulation only
Published source
Tian et al., Micromachines (2024), Tables 1–3; Figures 6–7
Problem
Compare three-point rigid-motion constraints with a fully fixed bottom face. Compute the hot and cold excursions from the paper’s 22 °C start and explain why a local TSV/RDL model needs a different mesh.
Numbers or observations to start from
Published thermal program: 22 °C start, 85 °C and −55 °C holds, repeated three times.
Evidence boundary
The reported fatigue life is calculated from simulation, not measured in this study. The paper’s modulus-table unit needs checking before reuse.

Flip-chip to BEOL: what does global validation cover?

Global measurement + local simulation
Published source
Li et al., Micromachines (2025), Tables 1–2; Figures 2–3
Problem
Map package, bump, BEOL, and wafer submodels. Identify which displacement boundary is transferred. Explain why a global warpage match does not by itself validate crack-tip energy release rate.
Numbers or observations to start from
The published package is 15 × 15 mm with a 5.48 × 5.84 mm die; global warpage was checked by shadow moiré.
Evidence boundary
Global warpage comparison and local fracture predictions have different evidence scopes.

Fine-pitch RDL: fabricated geometry versus simulated signaling

Measured geometry + SI simulation
Published source
Lee et al., Microelectronics (2026), Figures 5–8, 12–14
Problem
Check the critical-dimension tolerance for a 2 µm/2 µm RDL. Separate microscopy and FIB evidence from the channel eye diagrams. State the limitation created by approximating the HBM3 driver.
Numbers or observations to start from
The authors report measured line and space of 2.0 ± 0.5 µm (n = 8); the eye diagrams are simulated.
Evidence boundary
A fabricated line/space result does not constitute measured high-speed channel compliance; vendor IBIS data were unavailable.

Thin-wafer discrepancy: a digital-twin credibility card

Published experiment + credibility framework
Published source
IEEE TCPMT (2019) fan-out study; NIST digital-twin credibility guidance
Problem
Define specimen, stage, warpage metric, fixture, thickness range, model revision, and residuals. Specify one independent check that could distinguish a fixture/gravity effect from a model-material error.
Numbers or observations to start from
The IEEE abstract reports generally close agreement for its setup, alongside a substantial discrepancy for thin wafers.
Evidence boundary
Do not transfer the paper’s accuracy claim to another package or to InPack.AI. See NIST for intended-use credibility.

Integrated review and exam preparation

Explain the complete package-to-digital-twin workflow and defend a bounded design conclusion in written and oral form.

How this week works: Three approximately 56-minute modules plus two short transitions. Each hour has a distinct teaching target and a reviewable evidence checkpoint. InPack.AI is the course analysis platform; the handout records assumptions and outputs without inventing a software feature.

Public case: Cross-case review using public studies

Students compare the published fan-out warpage and two-chiplet thermal studies as separate specimens, then use the NIST credibility framework to write evidence-limited conclusions.

Evidence boundary: Cross-paper comparisons teach provenance; they do not create a single combined validated package.

Teaching modules

H1 · Rebuild the physical story

Outcome: Move from architecture and process to material inputs and local/global responses.

Evidence to keep: A complete but compact process-and-physics map.

Open H1 slidesSource notes

Architecture determines interfaces

Fan-out, interposer, and stacked packages have different routes and process gates.

Worked reasoning: Choose a package cross-section and identify die, bond, RDL or interposer, substrate, and cooling boundary.

Check your understanding: Can one generic stack represent every architecture?

No. Declare the actual layers and process.

Sources: R1 R3

History determines state

Cure, debond, grinding, and service loads may produce different shapes and stresses.

Worked reasoning: Use a published fan-out study to distinguish post-debond from post-grind warpage records.

Check your understanding: Can a final measurement substitute for every earlier stage?

No. Match the stage of the decision.

Sources: R8 R13

Coupled physics changes rankings

Routing, temperature, deformation, and testability can pull the design in different directions.

Worked reasoning: A synthetic layout shortens links but raises a hot spot; state the decision rule before ranking.

Check your understanding: Does one improved metric settle the design?

No. Check hard rules and the other critical metrics.

Sources: R2 R4

Materials need provenance

A number without grade, state, range, units, and source is an assumption rather than established material evidence.

Worked reasoning: Audit an EMC CTE entry against the paper's tested grade and process temperature.

Check your understanding: Can a value from one paper be reused unchanged?

Only with a justified material and condition match.

Sources: R6 R14

H2 · Review calculations and credibility

Outcome: Solve short design checks and state what they can and cannot prove.

Evidence to keep: A written response with calculation, assumptions, and validation plan.

Open H2 slidesSource notes

Calculate a thermal estimate

A lumped steady-state temperature rise equals heat flow times total thermal resistance under its assumptions.

Worked reasoning: Synthetic: 8 W through 2.5 K/W gives 20 K; then list the missing spatial hot-spot and transient effects.

Check your understanding: Does 20 K equal every die location?

No. It is a lumped estimate.

Sources: R4 R15

Calculate mismatch strain

Free thermal strain difference is approximately ΔαΔT for constant coefficients over the range.

Worked reasoning: Synthetic: 10 ppm/K difference across 100 K gives 0.001 free mismatch; do not equate it to local joint strain.

Check your understanding: Does free mismatch give fatigue life?

No. Geometry, constraints, constitutive law, and validation remain.

Sources: R7 R8

Separate V, V, and UQ

Code verification, physical validation, and uncertainty analysis answer distinct questions.

Worked reasoning: Classify mesh convergence, a warpage measurement, and CTE sensitivity into the three evidence categories.

Check your understanding: Does mesh convergence validate physical accuracy?

No. It checks numerical behavior.

Sources: R6

Check comparability before error

Prediction-minus-measurement is meaningful only for matched specimen, stage, metric, and conditions.

Worked reasoning: Reject a post-grind prediction paired with a post-debond measurement before computing percent error.

Check your understanding: Can an unmatched pair enter an average error?

No. State why it is incompatible.

Sources: R6 R8

H3 · Practice oral defense

Outcome: Give a concise claim, cite evidence, expose uncertainty, and answer a challenge.

Evidence to keep: A timed oral explanation and a revised evidence card.

Open H3 slidesSource notes

Lead with a narrow claim

A defensible statement names the package, output, conditions, and intended decision.

Worked reasoning: Say 'Candidate B predicts lower junction temperature at this power map' rather than 'B is best.'

Check your understanding: Why is 'best' too broad?

It hides criteria and untested conditions.

Sources: R2 R6

Show the strongest support

Use one sourced figure, calculation, or matched result with its units and definition.

Worked reasoning: Present the paper's process-stage comparison without transferring its accuracy to the classroom case.

Check your understanding: Can a literature result validate a different model automatically?

No. Specimen and setup must match.

Sources: R6 R8

Answer a counterexample

A good oral defense explains how a changed boundary or material could reverse a ranking.

Worked reasoning: An examiner changes TIM resistance; explain which calculation or measurement must be repeated.

Check your understanding: Should uncertainty be hidden to sound confident?

No. State the boundary and next test.

Sources: R4 R6

Close with the next test

A design recommendation remains provisional when a plausible missing measurement could change it.

Worked reasoning: Name the specimen, condition, measured quantity, and reversal rule for one package test.

Check your understanding: What completes the evidence loop?

A matched test, recorded residual, and updated bounded conclusion.

Sources: R6

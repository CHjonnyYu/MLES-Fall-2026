Packaging materials and interfaces

Select material properties for thermal and mechanical questions with temperature, process state, units, and provenance.

How this week works: Three approximately 56-minute teaching modules with 30 slides each, plus two short transitions. Each hour has a distinct teaching target and a reviewable evidence checkpoint. InPack.AI is the course analysis platform; the handout records assumptions and outputs without inventing a software feature.

Public case: NCKU fan-out EMC material sensitivity study

Chuang, Huang, and Chen publish a fan-out decarrier model with package dimensions, material ranges, a measurement comparison, and parameter sweeps. Its reported ranking belongs to that specimen and tested range.

Evidence boundary: Material cards vary by grade and process history. Classroom numerical exercises are synthetic.

Teaching modules

H1 · Properties are functions, not labels

Outcome: Choose the property needed for a particular package calculation and record its domain.

Evidence to keep: A material card with property, units, temperature range, state, and source.

Open H1 slides · 30 pagesSource notes

CTE describes expansion

A coefficient of thermal expansion links temperature change to free thermal strain over a stated range.

Worked reasoning: Synthetic arithmetic: 10 ppm/K over 100 K gives 0.001 free strain if the coefficient is constant.

Check your understanding: Does this predict package warpage by itself?

No. Constraint, layer geometry, and stiffness determine the assembled response.

Sources: R7 R8

Modulus controls load sharing

A stiffer layer can dominate composite bending even if it is thin.

Worked reasoning: Compare two equal-area layers with different moduli conceptually; identify which carries more in-plane force under equal strain.

Check your understanding: Can CTE alone rank warpage?

No. Stiffness and geometry also matter.

Sources: R7 R8

Conductivity needs a direction

Heat conduction can be anisotropic in layered or filled package materials.

Worked reasoning: Draw vertical heat escape through die, interface, and lid; mark which conductivity direction is relevant.

Check your understanding: Can an in-plane value automatically be used through thickness?

No. Verify anisotropy and measurement direction.

Sources: R4

Property tables have conditions

Record whether data are measured below or above transition, cured or uncured, dry or aged.

Worked reasoning: Reject a single room-temperature polymer modulus for a reflow calculation until its validity is checked.

Check your understanding: What should an absent high-temperature value say?

Missing; seek measurement or bound the model use.

Sources: R4 R7

H2 · Interfaces and process history

Outcome: Explain why bond, underfill, and encapsulant behavior depend on manufacturing sequence.

Evidence to keep: An interface map with stress-free state and likely failure locations.

Open H2 slides · 30 pagesSource notes

Interfaces have thermal resistance

A thin interface may dominate a thermal path if contact resistance is large.

Worked reasoning: Compare a bulk layer and contact on a heat-path sketch; identify the measurement needed to separate them.

Check your understanding: Does high bulk conductivity guarantee low junction temperature?

No. Interface resistance and boundary conditions matter.

Sources: R4

Underfill shares mechanical load

Underfill changes bump deformation and package stiffness; its cure state belongs in the model.

Worked reasoning: Mark the local displacement path at an outer bump and name the missing material curve.

Check your understanding: Can a generic underfill card validate joint strain?

No. Grade, cure, geometry, and test evidence are required.

Sources: R7

Cure creates a reference state

Cooling from cure can leave residual stress even before service loading.

Worked reasoning: Draw a stage diagram from molding to room-temperature inspection; identify where a stress-free assumption enters.

Check your understanding: Is room temperature automatically stress free?

No. The process history may already carry stress.

Sources: R7 R8

Solder behavior can be rate-dependent

Thermal cycling response can depend on time, temperature, and inelastic material behavior.

Worked reasoning: Contrast a quick elastic screening with a joint-lifetime claim; state which needs further constitutive data.

Check your understanding: May elastic stress alone establish fatigue life?

No. A validated damage relation and relevant history are needed.

Sources: R7

H3 · Build a defensible material card

Outcome: Audit one candidate material set before any simulation comparison.

Evidence to keep: A sourced property table and a missing-data plan.

Open H3 slides · 30 pagesSource notes

Never strip units from values

Unit conversion and temperature scale must be checked before a property enters a model.

Worked reasoning: Convert 12 ppm/K to 12 × 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹ and record the valid temperature range.

Check your understanding: Could 12 be entered as a dimensionless CTE?

No. That would be a million-fold input error.

Sources: R6 R7

State the source quality

A measured grade-specific curve, generic handbook value, and inferred placeholder support different claims.

Worked reasoning: Tag a missing EMC curve as a placeholder and run sensitivity only; do not call it measured.

Check your understanding: Does a plausible placeholder become evidence after a solver run?

No. Its provenance remains an assumption.

Sources: R6

Read a published CTE sensitivity

A one-factor model sweep estimates decision sensitivity within its specified specimen and process.

Worked reasoning: In the NCKU study, modeled X warpage changes from 7.340 to 6.431 mm when EMC lower-temperature CTE α₁ changes from 8 to 7 ppm/K. Calculate −0.909 mm and −12.4% relative to baseline.

Check your understanding: Does this sweep measure the true CTE of a new EMC?

No. It is this paper's model response to an assumed property change.

Sources: R6 R14

Choose a material measurement

Measure the uncertain property or package response that most affects the intended decision.

Worked reasoning: If interface resistance drives the temperature ranking, specify a coupon and matched thermal load.

Check your understanding: When should the ranking be withheld?

When plausible uncertainty reverses it or crosses a hard limit.

Sources: R4 R6

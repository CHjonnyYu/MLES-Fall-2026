Compare advanced package architectures

Compare fan-out, 2.5D interposer, and 3D stacking against a declared requirement and process constraints.

How this week works: Three approximately 56-minute teaching modules with 30 slides each, plus two short transitions. Each hour has a distinct teaching target and a reviewable evidence checkpoint. InPack.AI is the course analysis platform; the handout records assumptions and outputs without inventing a software feature.

Public case: Public technology descriptions and interconnect roadmap

Public TSMC architecture descriptions and IEEE interconnect roadmap support qualitative comparison. A synthetic decision matrix supplies classroom requirements, not vendor performance claims.

Evidence boundary: Technology categories do not fix cost, yield, temperature, or lifetime for a specific design.

Teaching modules

H1 · Recognize the physical architectures

Outcome: Classify package choices by die placement, interconnect layer, and vertical path.

Evidence to keep: Three annotated cross-sections and their key process steps.

Open H1 slides · 30 pagesSource notes

Fan-out uses redistribution

RDL can fan die connections outward within a molded package without requiring a conventional package substrate in that layer.

Worked reasoning: Trace a pad through RDL to an external joint in a conceptual fan-out cross-section.

Check your understanding: Is all fan-out identical?

No. Process flow, layer count, and materials vary.

Sources: R1 R3 R8

An interposer hosts dense links

A 2.5D interposer places dies side by side on a fine interconnect layer above a substrate.

Worked reasoning: Draw a compute-to-memory route and a separate vertical heat path.

Check your understanding: Does a fine interposer remove the substrate?

No. It serves a different layer of the package stack.

Sources: R1 R3

3D adds vertical interfaces

Stacking can shorten some links but intensifies thermal and test-access questions.

Worked reasoning: Mark where power and heat from an upper die must travel; identify one inspection challenge.

Check your understanding: Is a shorter interconnect always a better package?

No. Thermal, yield, power, and reliability constraints also apply.

Sources: R1 R3 R4

Names do not replace cross-sections

A vendor family name needs a physical description before a scientific comparison.

Worked reasoning: For each public option, annotate die positions, interconnect, substrate, and heat path before ranking.

Check your understanding: Can a marketing category establish a warpage value?

No. Geometry and materials are required.

Sources: R1 R2

H2 · Compare the constraints

Outcome: Build a decision matrix separating hard feasibility rules from performance objectives.

Evidence to keep: A requirement-to-architecture matrix with unknown entries left open.

Open H2 slides · 30 pagesSource notes

Start from required connections

I/O density and lane placement constrain interconnect choice but do not alone determine it.

Worked reasoning: Synthetic brief: a dense chiplet interface needs enough legal tracks; test each architecture with a stated rule.

Check your understanding: Does a nominal bump count prove routability?

No. Escape, layers, vias, and return paths need checks.

Sources: R2 R3

Trace the heat path

Stack height, interfaces, and die placement change thermal resistance and hot spots.

Worked reasoning: Compare a side-by-side and vertically stacked layout under the same assumed power and cooling.

Check your understanding: May different power loads be ranked by peak temperature?

No. Match loads or state the different use cases.

Sources: R4

Count assembly and inspection gates

Each added interface creates alignment, bonding, and test questions.

Worked reasoning: Build a gate list for a two-die interposer package and flag buried joints needing indirect tests.

Check your understanding: Does a final functional pass cover every latent defect?

No. Test coverage depends on fault and conditions.

Sources: R3 R12

Cost and yield require evidence

A general technology description does not supply a case-specific cost or yield model.

Worked reasoning: Mark cost as unknown until assumptions for die, assembly, test, and scrap are sourced.

Check your understanding: Can a qualitative 'lower cost' claim settle the matrix?

No. It needs a comparable scope and numbers.

Sources: R1 R2

H3 · Defend a bounded architecture choice

Outcome: Explain what is known, what is assumed, and which test could change the choice.

Evidence to keep: A one-page architecture decision record with two alternatives.

Open H3 slides · 30 pagesSource notes

Screen hard constraints first

An option violating a confirmed process rule needs redesign before performance ranking.

Worked reasoning: Synthetic A offers a short route but fails a declared keep-out; record the violation rather than awarding it a win.

Check your understanding: Should A win because its route is shorter?

No. Resolve the hard violation first.

Sources: R2 R3

Hold comparison conditions fixed

Power, geometry definition, ambient, and output metrics must be comparable across alternatives.

Worked reasoning: Compare two conceptual layouts at the same total power and cooling condition; label all numbers synthetic.

Check your understanding: Can a full-load result be ranked against idle?

No. Conditions differ.

Sources: R2 R6

Use a tradeoff, not one score

Routing, thermal, mechanical, test, and manufacturability evidence can conflict.

Worked reasoning: A side-by-side layout routes easily but is hotter at one die; choose only after stating the limit that matters.

Check your understanding: Does one lower maximum prove overall superiority?

No. Check other hard constraints and uncertainty.

Sources: R2 R4

Name a reversal test

The decision should state which missing measurement or rule could overturn it.

Worked reasoning: If the choice depends on interface resistance, request a matched coupon measurement before signoff.

Check your understanding: What makes the choice auditable?

Sources, assumptions, matched conditions, rule, uncertainty, and test.

Sources: R2 R6

AI-assisted package design

Use an AI proposal as a hypothesis generator while preserving physics checks, design rules, provenance, and held-out evaluation.

How this week works: Three approximately 56-minute modules plus two short transitions. Each hour has a distinct teaching target and a reviewable evidence checkpoint. InPack.AI is the course analysis platform; the handout records assumptions and outputs without inventing a software feature.

Public case: Published thermally aware placement research

The IEEE placement study illustrates an optimization research direction. It does not establish that the course has the same model, training data, performance, or InPack.AI implementation.

Evidence boundary: AI suggestions and public paper scores are not physical validation of a proposed package.

Teaching modules

H1 · Formulate the search problem

Outcome: Define variables, constraints, and objectives before asking an AI method to propose designs.

Evidence to keep: A formalized design-space card with rejection rules.

Open H1 slidesSource notes

A proposal is a candidate

An AI-generated placement is an input to engineering checks, not a signed-off design.

Worked reasoning: Sketch a proposed chiplet move; list one routing and one thermal check before accepting it.

Check your understanding: Does a plausible floorplan prove feasibility?

No. Physical constraints and operating conditions must be checked.

Sources: R2 R10

Encode discrete and continuous choices

Placement, rotation, stackup, and material selection have different legal domains.

Worked reasoning: Separate a continuous x-position from a discrete layer count and a fixed bump pitch rule.

Check your understanding: May an optimizer select an impossible layer count?

Only if the legal domain was omitted; reject and repair the formulation.

Sources: R2 R3

Choose meaningful objectives

Temperature, route length, and warpage are conditional metrics with units and conflicts.

Worked reasoning: Define a synthetic objective table but retain hard violations as separate pass/fail checks.

Check your understanding: Can a weighted score hide a failed hard rule?

Yes. Screen feasibility first.

Sources: R2 R4 R10

Document the evaluation model

Every AI score depends on a surrogate or solver and its tested domain.

Worked reasoning: Record whether a temperature is from a fast model, FEM run, or measurement; do not merge them.

Check your understanding: Is a model score a measured temperature?

No. Preserve the evidence type.

Sources: R6 R10

H2 · Evaluate candidates without leakage

Outcome: Keep independent test cases and physics checks separate from optimization feedback.

Evidence to keep: An evaluation plan with held-out conditions and failure cases.

Open H2 slidesSource notes

Optimization can exploit a weak model

A search algorithm may find designs where a surrogate is inaccurate rather than designs that are physically better.

Worked reasoning: If a candidate sits outside the model's training range, mark it out of domain and run a higher-fidelity check.

Check your understanding: Does a low surrogate score prove a good package?

No. Check fidelity and domain.

Sources: R6 R10

Hold out evaluation cases

Comparing on the same cases used to tune an AI method can overstate its benefit.

Worked reasoning: Split synthetic load maps by package configuration before tuning; evaluate once on distinct conditions.

Check your understanding: Can training fit be reported as independent accuracy?

No. It is not an independent test.

Sources: R6 R10

Use baselines with equal budgets

A claimed AI improvement needs a comparable manual or algorithmic baseline and equal constraints.

Worked reasoning: Compare two placement methods with the same package, power maps, allowed moves, and evaluation solver.

Check your understanding: Is a faster run automatically better?

No. Report solution quality, compute cost, and constraint compliance.

Sources: R2 R10

Probe failure regions

Report cases where routing, hot spots, or deformation worsen rather than only a best example.

Worked reasoning: Construct a synthetic close-placement case that reduces wire length but concentrates heat; explain the tradeoff.

Check your understanding: Should the failed case be omitted?

No. It bounds the method's use.

Sources: R4 R6 R10

H3 · Use InPack.AI evidence responsibly

Outcome: Audit actual platform exports if available; otherwise work from clearly labeled classroom records.

Evidence to keep: A candidate audit with source, version, constraints, and next physical test.

Open H3 slidesSource notes

Preserve platform provenance

An InPack.AI result needs actual case ID, input revision, method, conditions, and export; missing fields remain missing.

Worked reasoning: Compare a public paper with a course export in separate rows. If no export exists, use a labeled synthetic row.

Check your understanding: May a paper's result be relabeled as InPack.AI output?

No. They have different provenance.

Sources: R6

Reject unsupported claims

AI confidence, optimization score, and validation accuracy are different quantities.

Worked reasoning: Ask what a dashboard value measures before calling it temperature error or design confidence.

Check your understanding: Can a displayed percentage stand alone?

No. Define its denominator, reference, and evaluation data.

Sources: R6 R10

Keep a human decision gate

A reviewer should inspect hard constraints, uncertainty, and evidence before selecting a design.

Worked reasoning: An AI candidate has a good thermal score but no routing proof; request the missing rule check.

Check your understanding: Is a promising AI output ready for manufacture?

No. It needs the full design and process evidence.

Sources: R2 R6

Plan a falsifiable measurement

A physical test should target the predicted improvement under matched operating conditions.

Worked reasoning: Specify die power, sensor location, ambient, and candidate geometry for a thermal comparison.

Check your understanding: What would change the recommendation?

A matched test or verified hard constraint that contradicts the predicted advantage.

Sources: R6 R15

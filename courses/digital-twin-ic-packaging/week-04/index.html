Packaging process map and defect evidence

Trace a die through package assembly, locate where defects can enter, and document a bounded InPack.AI case record.

How this week works: Three approximately 56-minute teaching modules with 30 slides each, plus two short transitions. Each hour has a distinct teaching target and a reviewable evidence checkpoint. InPack.AI is the course analysis platform; the handout records assumptions and outputs without inventing a software feature.

Public case: Public assembly and inspection examples

The IEEE heterogeneous-integration roadmap provides public process and test context. Original classroom diagrams and values are conceptual or synthetic, not disclosed vendor process data.

Evidence boundary: An inspection indication is an observation; defect severity and cause still need a matched physical check.

Teaching modules

H1 · IC packaging process map

Outcome: Trace each major assembly operation, the physical state it changes, and its inspection gate.

Evidence to keep: An annotated process map from singulation to final test.

Open H1 slides · 30 pagesSource notes

Singulation changes a wafer into supported dies

Dicing separates dies while tape supports them; edge damage can precede package assembly.

Worked reasoning: Mark the saw street and identify where a chip or crack would be visible before placement.

Check your understanding: Why inspect the edge before die attach?

The operation can create local damage that becomes harder to attribute later.

Sources: R3 R12

Die attach creates a buried interface

The attach layer transfers mechanical load and heat; a void is a hypothesis until observed.

Worked reasoning: For a suspected void, distinguish acoustic indication from a measured heat-path consequence.

Check your understanding: Does a void indication prove thermal failure?

No. Its size, location, and effect need matched evidence.

Sources: R4 R12

First-level connections need different checks

Wire bonding and flip-chip joining have different joint geometry, access, and defect mechanisms.

Worked reasoning: Trace a signal through each route and name the hidden joint that the available inspection must address.

Check your understanding: Can a good wire loop image prove both bonds are strong?

No. Loop geometry and bond integrity are different observations.

Sources: R3 R12

Protection and test add new states

Molding, cure, cooling, and final test occur after the first join and may expose or introduce defects.

Worked reasoning: Follow a known-good die through molding to a final electrical open; list competing causes before diagnosis.

Check your understanding: Does final electrical failure reveal its root cause?

No. Additional stage-specific observations are needed.

Sources: R3 R12

H2 · Defects and inspection gates

Outcome: Match plausible defects to their physical observables, useful methods, and blind spots.

Evidence to keep: A five-column stage–defect–observable–method–decision map.

Open H2 slides · 30 pagesSource notes

Defects begin at different process stages

Edge chips, attach voids, bond damage, and encapsulation gaps point to distinct stages.

Worked reasoning: Assign an observed feature to the earliest plausible stage without treating timing alone as proof.

Check your understanding: Can a final open identify its stage by itself?

No. Several upstream mechanisms can produce an open.

Sources: R3 R12

Inspection methods observe different signals

Optical, X-ray, acoustic, and electrical tests have distinct contrast mechanisms and blind spots.

Worked reasoning: Choose acoustic inspection for a suspected buried interface gap, then state what it cannot quantify alone.

Check your understanding: Can an optical microscope see a buried attach void?

No. The hidden interface needs another suitable method.

Sources: R12

A post-reflow open needs a differential diagnosis

An observed electrical open is consistent with more than one joint or damage mechanism.

Worked reasoning: Compare a solder-joint open and bond damage; propose a test that could distinguish their locations.

Check your understanding: Should one inconclusive X-ray settle the cause?

No. Record its blind spot and use a complementary observation.

Sources: R3 R12

The defect map ends with a decision

Each row must connect stage, defect, observable, method, and action in that order.

Worked reasoning: Create two synthetic rows, including one test that cannot see the suspected feature.

Check your understanding: What makes a map reviewable?

A peer can identify the observation, method, blind spot, and decision rule.

Sources: R6 R12

H3 · InPack.AI evidence lab

Outcome: Keep case identity, inputs, predictions, observations, and limits separate in one engineering record.

Evidence to keep: A reproducible case card and a narrowly worded design claim.

Open H3 slides · 30 pagesSource notes

A platform case needs an identity

A reviewable InPack.AI case record should carry a case ID, input revision, material set, load, and export provenance.

Worked reasoning: If an instructor export is unavailable, write not supplied and use the clearly marked synthetic example.

Check your understanding: Can a public paper be relabeled as a platform run?

No. Preserve the original evidence type and provenance.

Sources: R6

Define every output before comparing

A model value needs quantity, unit, location, temperature, and process stage.

Worked reasoning: Check whether two warpage values refer to the same post-reflow 25 °C state before ranking them.

Check your understanding: Can a peak temperature be compared with an average?

No. Align metric definitions first.

Sources: R6 R8

Residuals describe a matched comparison

Measured minus predicted is interpretable only when specimen and conditions match.

Worked reasoning: Synthetic: A 92−80=+12 µm; B 64−60=+4 µm, both at 25 °C after reflow.

Check your understanding: Does B's smaller residual validate the model?

No. One synthetic pair cannot establish accuracy across a use domain.

Sources: R6 R8

A decision card exposes uncertainty

The final claim names its supporting result, known gaps, and the next physical test.

Worked reasoning: Recommend B only under the synthetic matched condition, then request a real matched measurement before release.

Check your understanding: What would make the claim stronger?

A traceable case export, representative measurement, uncertainty analysis, and checked design rules.

Sources: R6
